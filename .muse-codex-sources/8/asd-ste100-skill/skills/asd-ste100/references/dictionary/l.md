# ASD-STE100 Dictionary: Letter L

> **Governing Document**: ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English, Issue 9 (2025-01-15)  
> **Source Section**: Part 2 – Dictionary, Physical Pages 295–304 (Printed Pages 2-1-L1 through 2-1-L10)  
> **Scope**: Compact table reference for Letter L: 79 entries.

## 1. Navigational & Usage Overview

Letter **L** contains fundamental dimensional adjectives, spatial references, and mechanical operations. 

Critical structural traps in Letter L include:
- **Functional Shift prohibitions**: Words like `latch`, `light`, `load`, and `lock` have strict grammatical boundaries (e.g., `LATCH` is approved only as a noun; `LIGHT` is approved as noun and adjective, but prohibited as a verb; `LOWER` is approved as both adjective and verb).
- **The `LOOK AT` Phrasal Verb Exception**: Unlike standard prohibited phrasal verbs, **`LOOK AT (v)`** is explicitly approved in Issue 9 for visual inspection.

## 3. High-Frequency Pitfalls & Key Rules for Letter L

### 3.1 The `LATCH` vs `LOCK` Functional Boundary
- **`LATCH (n)`** is approved exclusively as a noun.
  - Non-STE: *Latch the access door.*
  - STE: `LOCK THE ACCESS DOOR.` or `CLOSE THE ACCESS DOOR.`
- Remember: you can touch a latch, examine a latch, or replace a latch, but you cannot *latch* anything.

### 3.2 The `LOOK AT (v)` Exception
- Rule 9.3 generally forbids phrasal verbs (*carry out*, *check out*, *back up*).
- **`LOOK AT (v)`** is one of the rare approved phrasal verbs in ASD-STE100, approved specifically to direct the operator's visual attention:
  - `LOOK AT THE PRESSURE GAUGE.`
  - `LOOK AT THE STATUS DISPLAY.`
- Do not use bare `look` without `at` (*Look if the light is on* $\rightarrow$ `MAKE SURE THAT THE LIGHT IS ON.`).
