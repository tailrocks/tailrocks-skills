## 1. Navigational & Usage Overview

Letter **O** contains foundational spatial and operational controls in ASD-STE100:
- **The `OBEY` vs `FOLLOW` Rule**: One of the most famous rules in STE. When dealing with instructions, procedures, or regulations, writers must use **`OBEY (v)`**. The verb `FOLLOW` is approved exclusively for physical/spatial movement ("come after / go after").
- **The `OVER` Prohibition**: In standard English, *over* is overloaded with dozens of meanings (above, finished, exceeding, across). In STE, *over* is completely unapproved: use **`ABOVE`** for position, **`MORE THAN`** for quantities, or **`ACROSS`** for movement.
- **Prefix `OVER-` Verbs**: Words like *overfill*, *overtighten*, and *overheat* must be written as analytical verb phrases: `FILL TOO MUCH`, `TIGHTEN TOO MUCH`, and `BECOME TOO HOT`.

## 3. High-Frequency Pitfalls & Key Rules for Letter O

### 3.1 The `OBEY` vs `FOLLOW` Mandate
- In standard English, people routinely say: *Follow these instructions.*
- In ASD-STE100, **`FOLLOW` means strictly physical/sequential movement**:
  - Permitted: `THE SECOND STEP FOLLOWS THE FIRST STEP.`
  - Permitted: `FOLLOW THE LINE OF RIVETS.`
- For instructions, checklists, rules, and procedures, authors **must use `OBEY`**:
  - Non-STE: *Follow the checklist.*
  - STE: `OBEY THE CHECKLIST.`
  - Non-STE: *Follow safety rules.*
  - STE: `OBEY SAFETY RULES.`

### 3.2 The Absolute Prohibition of `OVER`
- `over` is one of the most ambiguous prepositions in the English language. STE completely removes it from the vocabulary:
  - Non-STE: *Put the blanket over the engine.* $\rightarrow$ `PUT THE BLANKET ON THE ENGINE.`
  - Non-STE: *The temperature is over 100 °C.* $\rightarrow$ `THE TEMPERATURE IS MORE THAN 100 °C.`
  - Non-STE: *Install the cover over the gearbox.* $\rightarrow$ `INSTALL THE COVER ABOVE THE GEARBOX.`
  - Non-STE: *When the test is over...* $\rightarrow$ `WHEN THE TEST IS COMPLETE...`
