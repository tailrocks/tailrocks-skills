## 1. Navigational & Usage Overview

Letters **U** and **V** contain several of the most notorious high-frequency non-STE words in technical documentation:
- **The `UTILIZE` Prohibition**: Perhaps the most universally recognized plain-language violation. In STE, *utilize* is strictly prohibited; authors must always use **`USE (v)`**.
- **The `VERIFY` Prohibition**: Pervasive in test procedures and quality manuals. In STE, *verify* is unapproved; authors must use **`MAKE SURE (v)`**.
- **The `UNDER` Prohibition**: *under* is prohibited as a spatial preposition; authors must use **`BELOW`**.
- **Prefix `UN-` Discipline**: Unlisted negated adjectives/verbs (*unclip*, *uncap*, *undamaged*, *unpainted*) are prohibited and must be replaced by analytical forms (`NOT DAMAGED`, `REMOVE THE CAP`, `BARE`).

## 3. High-Frequency Pitfalls & Key Rules for Letters U & V

### 3.1 The `UTILIZE` and `VERIFY` Substitutions
- In technical prose, `utilize` and `verify` appear in almost every legacy engineering document.
- In ASD-STE100:
  - *utilize* $\rightarrow$ **`USE (v)`** (`USE A 1/2-INCH RATCHET.`)
  - *verify* $\rightarrow$ **`MAKE SURE (v)`** (`MAKE SURE THAT THE SWITCH IS OPEN.`)

### 3.2 The `UNDER` vs `BELOW` Mandate
- Standard English uses *under* interchangeably with *below*.
- STE prohibits *under* in spatial relationships:
  - Non-STE: *Install the drain line under the floor.*
  - STE: `INSTALL THE DRAIN LINE BELOW THE FLOOR.`
  - Non-STE: *Put the jack under the jacking pad.*
  - STE: `PUT THE JACK BELOW THE JACKING PAD.`

### 3.3 The `UPPER` vs `TOP` Mandate
- While **`LOWER`** is approved as an adjective, its opposite **`upper` is unapproved**.
- Writers must always pair `LOWER` with **`TOP`**:
  - Non-STE: *Remove the upper cover.*
  - STE: `REMOVE THE TOP COVER.`
  - STE: `REMOVE THE TOP AND LOWER ACCESS COVERS.`
