## 1. Navigational & Usage Overview

Letter **T** contains the most famous functional shift rule in Simplified Technical English:
- **The `TEST` Rule**: In ASD-STE100, **`TEST (n)` is approved ONLY as a noun**. The verb *test* is strictly prohibited. Technical actions must be written using **`DO A TEST (v)`** or nominal constructions.
- **The `TURN` vs `ROTATE` Rule**: **`TURN (v)`** is the sole approved verb for rotational and angular movement.
- **Phrasal Verbs with `TURN`**:
  - *turn on* $\rightarrow$ prohibited; use `SET TO ON` or `START`.
  - *turn off* $\rightarrow$ prohibited; use `SET TO OFF` or `SHUT DOWN`.
  - **`TURN OVER (v)`** $\rightarrow$ **APPROVED** phrasal verb exception meaning to invert or turn upside down.

## 3. High-Frequency Pitfalls & Key Rules for Letter T

### 3.1 The Absolute Prohibition of `TEST` as a Verb
- Under Rule 1.2 and Part 2, **`TEST` is strictly approved ONLY as a noun**.
- The verbal usage (*to test*) is one of the most penalizing violations in STE:
  - Non-STE: *Test the emergency lights.*
  - STE: `DO A TEST OF THE EMERGENCY LIGHTS.`
  - Non-STE: *Test the cable for continuity.*
  - STE: `DO A CONTINUITY TEST OF THE CABLE.`

### 3.2 The `TURN` vs `ROTATE` Rule
- `rotate` is never permitted in ASD-STE100 prose.
- Always use **`TURN`**:
  - Non-STE: *Rotate the drive shaft 90 degrees.*
  - STE: `TURN THE DRIVE SHAFT 90 DEGREES.`

### 3.3 The Phrasal Verb Discipline for `TURN`
- General phrasal verbs with `turn` (*turn on*, *turn off*, *turn out*, *turn up*) are strictly prohibited:
  - *Turn on the battery switch.* $\rightarrow$ `SET THE BATTERY SWITCH TO ON.`
  - *Turn off the generator.* $\rightarrow$ `SET THE GENERATOR TO OFF.` or `SHUT DOWN THE GENERATOR.`
- **Authorized Exception**: **`TURN OVER (v)`** is explicitly approved when inverting an object:
  - `TURN OVER THE ACCESS DOOR TO EXAMINE THE INSIDE SURFACE.`
