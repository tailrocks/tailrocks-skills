## 1. Principles of Verb Selection in ASD-STE100

ASD-STE100 strictly limits the general action verbs permitted in technical authoring. Key grammatical and semantic principles include:

1. **Closed Vocabulary**: Only verbs explicitly listed in the dictionary or authorized as domain-specific Technical Verbs (TV, Rule 1.12) are permitted. If an action verb is not approved, use an approved alternative or rephrase.
2. **Morphological Restrictions**: Writers must use only the verb forms explicitly listed in the dictionary entry (Infinitive/Imperative, 3rd Person Present, Past, Past Participle). If a form is not listed (or noted as *No other verb forms*), it cannot be used.
3. **Permitted Tenses (Rule 2.1)**:
   - Imperative mood (commands/procedures): `DISCONNECT THE CABLE.`
   - Present tense (descriptions/facts): `THE VALVE CONTROLS THE FLOW.`
   - Simple past tense (completed actions): `THE FLUID FLOWED INTO THE TANK.`
   - Simple future tense with `WILL`: `THE SYSTEM WILL SHUT DOWN.`
   - Continuous/progressive forms (`-ing`) and complex compound past tenses (`had been doing`) are **strictly prohibited**.
4. **Active Voice (Rule 2.2)**: Always write in active voice. Passive voice is prohibited in procedural text and heavily restricted in descriptive text.
5. **One Meaning per Verb (Rule 1.3)**: Approved verbs have exactly one core approved technical sense (or strictly defined secondary numbered senses). Do not use an approved verb with a colloquial or unapproved meaning.
