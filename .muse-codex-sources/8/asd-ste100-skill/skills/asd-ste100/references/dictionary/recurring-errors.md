# List of Recurring Errors in ASD-STE100

> **Governing Specification**: ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English, Issue 9 (2025-01-15)  
> **Source Location**: Part 2 – Dictionary, Pages 2-0-17 and 2-0-18 (Physical Pages 145–146)  
> **Authority**: Aerospace, Security and Defence Industries Association of Europe (ASD)  
> **Scope**: Complete Catalog of the Most Frequent Non-STE Lexical Errors, Approved Alternatives, and Replacement Guidance

---

## 1. Executive Summary & The STEMG Catalog Rationale

The ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English Maintenance Group (STEMG) publishes an official catalog of recurring errors at the beginning of Part 2. Through decades of analyzing aerospace, defense, and industrial documentation, the STEMG identified that the overwhelming majority of non-compliant sentences stem from a recurring set of approximately 40 common English words.

These errors primarily fall into five linguistic failure modes:
1. **Functional Shift (Conversion)**: Using an approved noun as a verb (`check (v)`, `test (v)`, `damage (v)`, `cover (v)`).
2. **Modal Auxiliary Confusion**: Using subjective or ambiguous modal auxiliaries (`shall`, `should`, `may`, `need`, `have to`) instead of the mandatory STE forms `MUST`, `CAN`, or imperative verbs.
3. **Vague Descriptive Adjectives**: Using colloquial or multi-meaning adjectives (`acceptable`, `alternate`, `complete`, `main`, `old`) instead of exact technical descriptors (`PERMITTED`, `ALTERNATIVE`, `COMPLETED`, `PRIMARY`, `USED/REMAINING/EXPIRED`).
4. **Prepositional & Conjunction Ambiguity**: Using multi-sense prepositions or conjunctions (`over`, `under`, `since`, `however`, `therefore`) where causality, spatial location, or quantity become conflated.
5. **Gerunds & Participle Shorthand**: Using `-ing` verbals (`using`, `following`) to introduce procedural steps, violating the strict requirement for finite active verbs and chronological imperatives.

## 3. Detailed Guidance and Examples for Each Recurring Error

### 3.1 `acceptable (adj)` -> `PERMITTED (adj)`
- **Problem**: `acceptable` expresses subjective judgment rather than objective technical compliance.
- **Approved STE**: `PERMITTED (adj)` or `SATISFACTORY (adj)`.
- **Non-STE Example**: *A maximum clearance of 0.5 mm is acceptable.*
- **Approved STE Example**: `A MAXIMUM CLEARANCE OF 0.5 mm IS PERMITTED.`

### 3.2 `alternate (adj)` -> `ALTERNATIVE (adj)`
- **Problem**: In standard English, `alternate` means occurring by turns or every other one. Authors often use it incorrectly to mean an optional replacement.
- **Approved STE**: `ALTERNATIVE (adj)`.
- **Non-STE Example**: *Use an alternate seal if the primary seal is not available.*
- **Approved STE Example**: `USE AN ALTERNATIVE SEAL IF THE PRIMARY SEAL IS NOT AVAILABLE.`

### 3.3 `any (adj)` -> Omission or Sentence Reconstruction
- **Problem**: `any` introduces vagueness, non-determinism, and translation ambiguity.
- **Approved STE**: Frequently, omit the word completely, or rewrite using `ALL`, `A`, or an `IF` condition.
- **Non-STE Example**: *Examine the cable for any signs of damage.*
- **Approved STE Example**: `EXAMINE THE CABLE FOR SIGNS OF DAMAGE.`
- **Help Notice (Category 4)**: *"Frequently, an alternative for this word is not necessary."*

### 3.4 `avoid (v)` -> `PREVENT (v)`
- **Problem**: `avoid` implies passive evasion rather than proactive engineering prevention.
- **Approved STE**: `PREVENT (v)`.
- **Non-STE Example**: *To avoid contamination of the fuel system, install protective caps.*
- **Approved STE Example**: `TO PREVENT CONTAMINATION OF THE FUEL SYSTEM, INSTALL PROTECTIVE CAPS.`

### 3.5 `both (adj)` -> `THE TWO (TN)`
- **Problem**: `both` can create parsing ambiguity in complex noun phrases and translated target languages.
- **Approved STE**: `THE TWO (TN)` or explicit enumeration.
- **Non-STE Example**: *Disconnect both hydraulic lines.*
- **Approved STE Example**: `DISCONNECT THE TWO HYDRAULIC LINES.`

### 3.6 `check (v)` -> `CHECK (n)`, `EXAMINE (v)`, `MAKE SURE (v)`, `MEASURE (v)`
- **Problem**: `CHECK` is approved **only as a noun** `(n)` in STE. Using `check` as an action verb is one of the single most frequent violations in technical writing.
- **Approved STE**:
  - For verification: `MAKE SURE (v)` (`MAKE SURE THAT IT IS SAFE TO SUPPLY ELECTRICAL POWER.`)
  - For dimensions/tolerances: `MEASURE (v)` (`MEASURE THE DISTANCE BETWEEN THE SURFACES.`)
  - For physical inspection: `EXAMINE (v)` (`EXAMINE THE CASTING FOR CORROSION.`)
  - As a procedure noun: `CHECK (n)` (`DO A LEAKAGE CHECK OF THE VALVE.`)
- **Non-STE Example**: *Check the valve for leakage.*
- **Approved STE Example**: `DO A CHECK OF THE VALVE FOR LEAKAGE.`

### 3.7 `cover (v)` -> `COVER (TN)`, `INCLUDE (v)`, `HAVE (v)`, `PUT ON (v)`
- **Problem**: `cover` is unapproved as a verb. It creates functional shift ambiguity.
- **Approved STE**:
  - Mechanical protection: `PUT A COVER ON` or `INSTALL A COVER`
  - Document scope: `INCLUDE (v)` or `HAVE (v)`
- **Non-STE Example**: *This manual covers maintenance of the auxiliary power unit.*
- **Approved STE Example**: `THIS MANUAL GIVES MAINTENANCE INSTRUCTIONS FOR THE AUXILIARY POWER UNIT.` / `THIS MANUAL INCLUDES DATA ABOUT...`

### 3.8 `complete (adj)` -> `COMPLETED (adj)`, `FULL (adj)`, `ALL (adj)`
- **Problem**: Authors confuse the base adjective `complete` with the past participle adjective `COMPLETED`.
- **Approved STE**: `COMPLETED (adj)` (status of an action) or `FULL (adj)` / `ALL (adj)` (totality).
- **Non-STE Example**: *When the test is complete, disconnect the test set.*
- **Approved STE Example**: `WHEN THE TEST IS COMPLETED, DISCONNECT THE TEST SET.`

### 3.9 `damage (v)` -> `DAMAGE (n)`, `CAUSE (v)`
- **Problem**: `DAMAGE` is approved **only as a noun** `(n)`. Converting it to a verb (`do not damage...`) is prohibited.
- **Approved STE**: `CAUSE (v) DAMAGE TO...` or `DAMAGE (n)`.
- **Non-STE Example**: *Do not damage the O-ring during installation.*
- **Approved STE Example**: `DO NOT CAUSE DAMAGE TO THE O-RING DURING INSTALLATION.`

### 3.10 `ensure (v)` -> `MAKE SURE (v)`
- **Problem**: `ensure` is common in legacy aerospace manuals but is completely unapproved in STE.
- **Approved STE**: `MAKE SURE (v)`.
- **Non-STE Example**: *Ensure that the circuit breaker is open.*
- **Approved STE Example**: `MAKE SURE THAT THE CIRCUIT BREAKER IS OPEN.`

### 3.11 `fit (v)` -> `INSTALL (v)`, `ATTACH (v)`, `CORRECT (adj)`
- **Problem**: `fit` has multiple colloquial meanings (to install, to be suitable, to be the right size).
- **Approved STE**: `INSTALL (v)` or `ATTACH (v)`.
- **Non-STE Example**: *Fit the gasket to the mating flange.*
- **Approved STE Example**: `INSTALL THE GASKET ON THE MATING FLANGE.`

### 3.12 `follow (v)` -> `OBEY (v)`
- **Problem**: In STE, `FOLLOW` is approved **only** for physical movement in sequence (`FOLLOW THE TRACTOR TO THE RUNWAY.`). When referring to instructions, manuals, or warnings, you must use `OBEY`.
- **Approved STE**: `OBEY (v)`.
- **Non-STE Example**: *Follow the manufacturer instructions.*
- **Approved STE Example**: `OBEY THE MANUFACTURER INSTRUCTIONS.`

### 3.13 `further (adj/adv)` -> `MORE (adj/adv)`
- **Problem**: `further` is an unapproved irregular comparative.
- **Approved STE**: `MORE (adj)` or `MORE (adv)`.
- **Non-STE Example**: *For further information, refer to Chapter 20.*
- **Approved STE Example**: `FOR MORE DATA, REFER TO CHAPTER 20.`

### 3.14 `have to (v)` -> Direct Imperative Command or `MUST (v)`
- **Problem**: `have to` is wordy, conversational, and dilutes procedural force.
- **Approved STE**: Direct action verb in the imperative mood (procedures) or `MUST` (requirements).
- **Non-STE Example**: *You have to remove the access panel before testing.*
- **Approved STE Example**: `REMOVE THE ACCESS PANEL BEFORE YOU DO THE TEST.`

### 3.15 `however (adv)` -> `BUT (conj)`
- **Problem**: Authors frequently write complex compound sentences with semicolons and `however`, creating run-on sentences that violate Rule 4.1.
- **Approved STE**: `BUT (conj)` (simple conjunction).
- **Non-STE Example**: *The valve opened; however, the pressure did not decrease.*
- **Approved STE Example**: `THE VALVE OPENED, BUT THE PRESSURE DID NOT DECREASE.`

### 3.16 `insert (v)` -> `PUT (v)`
- **Problem**: `insert` is a Latinate synonym for `put`. STE standardizes on the simple verb `PUT`.
- **Approved STE**: `PUT (v)`.
- **Non-STE Example**: *Insert the locking pin into the hole.*
- **Approved STE Example**: `PUT THE LOCKING PIN INTO THE HOLE.`

### 3.17 `main (adj)` -> `PRIMARY (adj)`
- **Problem**: `main` is colloquial and imprecise.
- **Approved STE**: `PRIMARY (adj)`.
- **Non-STE Example**: *Disconnect the main fuel supply hose.*
- **Approved STE Example**: `DISCONNECT THE PRIMARY FUEL SUPPLY HOSE.`

### 3.18 `may (v)` -> `CAN (v)`, `POSSIBLY (adv)`
- **Problem**: `may` is notoriously ambiguous in technical specifications: does it mean permission, possibility, or weak obligation?
- **Approved STE**: `CAN (v)` for capability/possibility; `POSSIBLY (adv)` for uncertainty.
- **Non-STE Example**: *Hydraulic fluid may cause skin irritation.*
- **Approved STE Example**: `HYDRAULIC FLUID CAN CAUSE SKIN IRRITATION.`

### 3.19 `need (v)` -> `NECESSARY (adj)`
- **Problem**: `need` implies personal human requirement rather than objective technical necessity.
- **Approved STE**: `NECESSARY (adj)`.
- **Non-STE Example**: *You need to clean the filter element every 500 hours.*
- **Approved STE Example**: `IT IS NECESSARY TO CLEAN THE FILTER ELEMENT EVERY 500 HOURS.`

### 3.20 `now (adv)` -> `AT THIS TIME`
- **Problem**: `now` is conversational and often redundant in procedural steps.
- **Approved STE**: `AT THIS TIME` or omission.
- **Non-STE Example**: *You can now open the bypass valve.*
- **Approved STE Example**: `YOU CAN OPEN THE BYPASS VALVE AT THIS TIME.`

### 3.21 `old (adj)` -> `REMAINING (adj)`, `USED (adj)`, `EXPIRED (adj)`
- **Problem**: `old` is completely subjective. Does it mean age, wear, expiration, or pre-existing?
- **Approved STE**: `REMAINING (adj)`, `USED (adj)`, or `EXPIRED (adj)`.
- **Non-STE Example**: *Discard the old seal and install a new one.*
- **Approved STE Example**: `DISCARD THE USED SEAL AND INSTALL A NEW SEAL.`

### 3.22 `over (prep)` -> `ABOVE (prep)`, `ON (prep)`, `ALONG (prep)`, `MORE THAN`
- **Problem**: In Issue 9, `over` was shifted to **unapproved** because it conflates spatial position, movement path, and numerical excess.
- **Approved STE**:
  - Spatial elevation: `ABOVE (prep)`
  - Surface contact: `ON (prep)`
  - Trajectory: `ALONG (prep)`
  - Quantitative threshold: `MORE THAN`
- **Non-STE Example**: *The temperature must not be over 120 °C.*
- **Approved STE Example**: `THE TEMPERATURE MUST NOT BE MORE THAN 120 °C.`

### 3.23 `people (n)` -> `PERSON (n)`, `PERSONNEL (n)`
- **Problem**: `people` is an irregular plural noun associated with general society.
- **Approved STE**: `PERSON (n)` (individuals) or `PERSONNEL (n)` (workforce/crew).
- **Non-STE Example**: *Make sure that no people are in the danger area.*
- **Approved STE Example**: `MAKE SURE THAT NO PERSONNEL ARE IN THE DANGER AREA.`

### 3.24 `perform (v)` -> `DO (v)`, `MEASURE (v)`
- **Problem**: `perform` is an unnecessary nominalization auxiliary (`perform an inspection`).
- **Approved STE**: `DO (v)` or direct action verb.
- **Non-STE Example**: *Perform the functional test of the elevator.*
- **Approved STE Example**: `DO THE FUNCTIONAL TEST OF THE ELEVATOR.`

### 3.25 `portion (n)` -> `PART (n)`, `PIECE (n)`
- **Problem**: `portion` is literary; STE uses `PART` or `PIECE`.
- **Approved STE**: `PART (n)` or `PIECE (n)`.
- **Non-STE Example**: *Cut off the damaged portion of the hose.*
- **Approved STE Example**: `CUT OFF THE DAMAGED PART OF THE HOSE.`

### 3.26 `press (v)` -> `PUSH (v)`
- **Problem**: `press` is unapproved as a verb for operating controls or buttons.
- **Approved STE**: `PUSH (v)`.
- **Non-STE Example**: *Press the EMERGENCY STOP switch.*
- **Approved STE Example**: `PUSH THE EMERGENCY STOP SWITCH.`

### 3.27 `reach (v)` -> `GET (v)`, `TOUCH (v)`, `BE (v)`
- **Problem**: `reach` is ambiguous between physical touching, arriving at a destination, and attaining a numerical value.
- **Approved STE**: `GET (v)` (numerical threshold) or `TOUCH (v)` (contact).
- **Non-STE Example**: *Wait until the oil pressure reaches 400 kPa.*
- **Approved STE Example**: `WAIT UNTIL THE OIL PRESSURE GETS TO 400 kPa.`

### 3.28 `repeat (v)` -> `DO (v) ... AGAIN`
- **Problem**: `repeat` is unapproved in STE.
- **Approved STE**: `DO (v) ... AGAIN`.
- **Non-STE Example**: *Repeat step 2 until the fluid is clear.*
- **Approved STE Example**: `DO STEP 2 AGAIN UNTIL THE FLUID IS CLEAR.`

### 3.29 `required (v/adj)` -> `NECESSARY (adj)`
- **Problem**: `required` is frequently used in passive shorthand (`as required`), which obscures who requires the action and what standard applies.
- **Approved STE**: `NECESSARY (adj)`.
- **Non-STE Example**: *Adjust the tension as required.*
- **Approved STE Example**: `ADJUST THE TENSION WHEN NECESSARY.`

### 3.30 `rotate (v)` -> `TURN (v)`
- **Problem**: `rotate` is Latinate; STE uses the Germanic core verb `TURN`.
- **Approved STE**: `TURN (v)`.
- **Non-STE Example**: *Rotate the crankshaft three full revolutions.*
- **Approved STE Example**: `TURN THE CRANKSHAFT THREE FULL REVOLUTIONS.`

### 3.31 `secure (v)` -> `ATTACH (v)`, `LOCK WITH SAFETY WIRE`
- **Problem**: `secure` is dangerously ambiguous in aerospace: does it mean fastening a bolt, closing a door, or lockwiring a component?
- **Approved STE**: `ATTACH (v)` (mechanical fastening) or
  `LOCK WITH SAFETY WIRE` (lockwire/cotter pin).
- **Non-STE Example**: *Secure the panel with ten screws.*
- **Approved STE Example**: `ATTACH THE PANEL WITH TEN SCREWS.`
- **Non-STE Example**: *Secure the turnbuckle with safety wire.*
- **Approved STE Example**: `LOCK THE TURNBUCKLE WITH SAFETY WIRE.`

### 3.32 `shall (v)` -> `MUST (v)`
- **Problem**: `shall` is obsolete in plain English and absent from STE.
- **Approved STE**: `MUST (v)`.
- **Non-STE Example**: *The operator shall inspect the filter daily.*
- **Approved STE Example**: `THE OPERATOR MUST EXAMINE THE FILTER DAILY.`

### 3.33 `should (v)` -> `MUST (v)` or Explicit Advisory
- **Problem**: `should` creates fatal ambiguity: is the action mandatory, strongly advised, or purely optional?
- **Approved STE**: `MUST (v)` if mandatory; if an advisory, rewrite with `WE RECOMMEND THAT...`.
- **Non-STE Example**: *You should replace worn bolts immediately.*
- **Approved STE Example**: `YOU MUST REPLACE WORN BOLTS IMMEDIATELY.`

### 3.34 `since (conj)` -> `BECAUSE (conj)`
- **Problem**: In standard English, `since` can mean both "from that time" and "because". In STE, `SINCE` is restricted to time only.
- **Approved STE**: `BECAUSE (conj)` (for causality).
- **Non-STE Example**: *Since the seal was damaged, fuel leaked into the cowl.*
- **Approved STE Example**: `BECAUSE THE SEAL WAS DAMAGED, FUEL LEAKED INTO THE COWL.`

### 3.35 `test (v)` -> `TEST (n)`, `DO (v)`
- **Problem**: `TEST` is approved **only as a noun** `(n)`. Converting it into an action verb (`test the circuit`) is prohibited.
- **Approved STE**: `DO A TEST OF...` or `DO A FUNCTIONAL TEST...`.
- **Non-STE Example**: *Test the auxiliary generator before flight.*
- **Approved STE Example**: `DO A TEST OF THE AUXILIARY GENERATOR BEFORE FLIGHT.`

### 3.36 `therefore (adv)` -> `THUS (adv)`, `AS A RESULT`
- **Problem**: `therefore` is overly formal and often introduces complex sentence structures.
- **Approved STE**: `THUS (adv)` or `AS A RESULT`.
- **Non-STE Example**: *The valve was stuck; therefore, pressure increased.*
- **Approved STE Example**: `THE VALVE WAS STUCK. AS A RESULT, THE PRESSURE INCREASED.`

### 3.37 `under (prep)` -> `BELOW (prep)`, `IN (prep)`, `LESS THAN`
- **Problem**: `under` conflates physical position, environmental conditions, and numerical minimums.
- **Approved STE**:
  - Spatial: `BELOW (prep)` (`BELOW THE WING`)
  - Environmental: `IN (prep)` (`IN ADVERSE CONDITIONS`)
  - Numerical: `LESS THAN` (`LESS THAN 50 kPa`)
- **Non-STE Example**: *Operate the engine under low load.*
- **Approved STE Example**: `OPERATE THE ENGINE IN LOW LOAD CONDITIONS.`
- **Non-STE Example**: *Do not operate when the temperature is under 0 °C.*
- **Approved STE Example**: `DO NOT OPERATE WHEN THE TEMPERATURE IS LESS THAN 0 °C.`

### 3.38 `using (v)` -> `USE (v)`, `WITH (prep)`
- **Problem**: Technical authors frequently use `using` in
  participial dangling phrases (`Remove the nut using a wrench`),
  violating Rule 3.5 (no `-ing` verb forms) and Rule 4.1.
- **Approved STE**: Split into chronological steps with imperative `USE`, or use preposition `WITH`.
- **Non-STE Example**: *Remove the bracket using a screwdriver.*
- **Approved STE Example**: `USE A SCREWDRIVER TO REMOVE THE BRACKET.` / `REMOVE THE BRACKET WITH A SCREWDRIVER.`

## 4. Summary Checklist for Quality Assurance

Before releasing any technical text, run this rapid compliance audit against the STEMG Recurring Errors table:

1. [ ] Are all instances of `check` and `test` used strictly as nouns (e.g., `do a check`, `do a test`)?
2. [ ] Are all obligations stated with `MUST` or direct imperatives, with zero instances of `shall`, `should`, or `have to`?
3. [ ] Are all causal clauses introduced by `BECAUSE`, with `since` used only for elapsed time?
4. [ ] Are all spatial positions expressed with `ABOVE` and `BELOW`, with zero instances of `over` or `under`?
5. [ ] Are numerical thresholds stated as `MORE THAN` and `LESS THAN`?
6. [ ] Are physical installations directed by `INSTALL` or `ATTACH`, with zero instances of `fit` or `secure`?
7. [ ] Are procedural steps introduced by active finite verbs, with zero trailing `using...` participles?
