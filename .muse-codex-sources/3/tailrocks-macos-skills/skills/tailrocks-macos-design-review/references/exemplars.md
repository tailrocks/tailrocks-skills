# Exemplar doctrine

Study structure, restraint, hierarchy, behavior, and accessibility. Never copy
subject matter, product identity, or decorative treatment. The corpus is an
internal design instrument, not a provenance archive.

## Reference shapes

Choose examples by the work shape they solve:

| Product shape | Inspect |
|---|---|
| Document or browser | restrained toolbar grouping, content under chrome, tabs, search, restoration |
| Media library | full-bleed content only where the backdrop is curated and controls stay legible |
| Canvas or editor | controls floating above the canvas without becoming content decoration |
| Spatial canvas | overlay opacity, concentric geometry, and unobstructed direct manipulation |
| Dense grid or schedule | hard scroll-edge separation, keyboard traversal, and compact density |
| Files or records browser | restrained sidebar/table/inspector composition and stable selection |
| Pro tool | consolidated chrome, customizable commands, multiple windows, and long-value placement |

## Positive mechanisms

- Standard components own material, accessibility substitutions, focus, and
  interaction whenever they satisfy the job.
- Functional glass is sparse and floats above content. Content remains plain.
- Unpredictable content behind controls demands the more legible system variant.
- Dense interfaces spend visual emphasis on the active job, not every action.
- Canvas tools remain reachable while leaving the canvas visually dominant.
- Search, inspector, toolbar, sidebar, table, and window behavior follow their
  platform roles before receiving product-specific composition.

## Counter-examples

- Transparent controls disappear against changing imagery.
- Mobile navigation is enlarged into desktop chrome.
- Equal-card dashboards erase a dominant workflow.
- Each action becomes a tinted capsule, leaving no hierarchy.
- Custom blur imitates system material and misses accessibility substitution.
- A fixed canvas, modal dependency, or hover-only action breaks desktop work.

## Admission rule

An exemplar enters a product corpus only with a concrete screen/job annotation,
the mechanism worth learning, accessibility and alternate-state evidence, what
transfers, and what must not be copied. Unverified praise, screenshots without
analysis, and attribution-only notes do not enter.
