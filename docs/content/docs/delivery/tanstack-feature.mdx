---
title: "Worked example: a TanStack feature on a Rust backend"
description: "Offline inspection capture with a live supervisor board — the same pipeline on a brownfield product, where research carries most of the weight."
---

The product: compliance software for fire-safety inspection contractors. The
form renderer, scheduling, and report generator already exist.

The feature: **offline inspection capture with a live site board.** An inspector
works a whole job on a tablet with no connectivity — verdict per asset, defect
severity, notes, photos, signature. On large sites two inspectors split zones
and work the *same* inspection concurrently. Work syncs when the network
returns, and an office supervisor watches progress stream in and adjudicates
anything that diverged before the certificate is signed.

TypeScript in the browser, Axum behind it. This example is worth walking for a
different reason than [the macOS one](/docs/delivery/macos-app): almost every
hard question here has a factual answer somebody has to go and find, which is
what `tailrocks-research` exists for — and the two questions that *don't* are
the ones that decide the product.

## 1. Capture — `tailrocks-idea`

```text
Use tailrocks-idea: inspectors need to complete inspections with no signal in
basements and plant rooms, and the office needs to see progress as it happens.
```

Slug `offline-inspection`, status DRAFT. Note what the sentence does not say:
nothing about two inspectors on one job, nothing about photos, nothing about
what "as it happens" means when a device has been in a basement for two hours.
Those gaps are the next two stages.

## 2. Shape — `tailrocks-brainstorm`

```text
Use tailrocks-brainstorm on offline-inspection.
```

The interview goes after the tensions, not the features. On this item it lands
on five questions that no amount of research can settle, because each is a
product position:

1. Two inspectors record **different verdicts** for the same fire door while
   offline. Does the later write win, or is divergence a hard conflict that
   blocks the certificate until a supervisor adjudicates?
2. Is an inspection legally submitted at the moment of **offline signature**, or
   at server ingest? This one answer decides which timestamp is printed on a
   compliance certificate.
3. What is the maximum supported **offline window** — one shift, 24 hours, a
   week, unbounded?
4. Does the supervisor board show **unsynced draft verdicts**, or only committed
   ones? Drafts are more useful and create real liability when a supervisor acts
   on a "fail" the inspector then revises.
5. Under storage pressure mid-inspection, does the app **degrade** — drop photo
   resolution, force a sync-or-stop — or hard-block new capture?

Brainstorm does not answer these. It writes them into the item, recommends a
position on each, and asks. Meanwhile it looks up anything lookup-able itself
rather than spending your attention on it.

## 3. Establish facts — `tailrocks-research`

Three topics, each genuinely unanswerable from the armchair, each reusable by
the next feature that touches the same ground.

```text
Use tailrocks-research on offline-inspection: which CRDT gives us a Rust
server-side replica and a browser client sharing one wire format, and can it
surface that two updates disagreed rather than silently merging them?
```

That last clause is the whole point, and it is the kind of thing a shaping
interview only surfaces if someone is looking for it. **A CRDT is designed to
eliminate conflicts.** Feed it two contradictory pass/fail verdicts and it
returns one deterministic answer — the math is right and the artifact is wrong,
because nobody signed the merged document. The feature needs conflict
*detection* layered on top of a library built to make conflicts impossible.
Candidates the topic weighs: Loro, Yjs with its Rust counterpart Yrs, and
Automerge, whose change history maps well onto "who claimed what, when".

```text
Use tailrocks-research on offline-inspection: what durability can we actually
promise for offline data in a browser on iOS, and what does requesting
persistent storage genuinely grant?
```

This one returns bad news, which is exactly why it runs before planning rather
than during it: Safari evicts origin storage after a week without interaction,
quota is opaque, and iOS supports neither Background Sync nor Periodic
Background Sync — so there is no way to flush a queue after the tab closes.
Losing three hours of capture is a product-killing failure, and the fallback
(foreground-only flush, an explicit "safe to close" affordance) is a *design*
consequence of a *platform* fact.

```text
Use tailrocks-research on offline-inspection: which resumable upload protocol
for the photo pipeline, and what is the real server-side story in Rust?
```

Photos are 30–80 files at several megabytes each. They cannot ride the document
channel, which means the document will reference blobs that have not uploaded
yet — a dangling-reference window that can last hours and that the supervisor's
live view will see. The topic compares the established tus protocol against the
IETF resumable-upload draft, and finds the Rust server-side ecosystem thin
enough that implementing the upload resource directly in Axum is the likely
answer.

```text
+ research/offline-crdt-selection/
+ research/browser-offline-durability/
+ research/resumable-evidence-upload/
~ roadmap/offline-inspection/README.md   Research links
```

Every claim carries a primary source. The orchestrator opens each citation and
confirms it supports the claim — misattributions get fixed, unverifiable claims
get dropped. Topics stay in `research/`, not buried in a plan folder, because
the next offline feature in this product will need all three.

## 4. Decide — `tailrocks-record-decision`

Now the five questions are answerable, because the research changed what is
possible.

```text
Use tailrocks-record-decision on offline-inspection: divergent verdicts on the
same asset are a hard conflict that blocks certificate generation until a
supervisor adjudicates, because a merged verdict is one nobody signed.
```

```text
Use tailrocks-record-decision on offline-inspection: every event carries both
device time and server time in separate columns, never coalesced, because an
offline tablet's clock is untrusted and inspection times are legally material.
```

One decision per invocation. Each is dated with its reason, propagated through
the item, and turned into Must-not entries where it forbids something. The
second one above is the sort of constraint that is invisible in a schema review
six months later and obvious in the item's Decisions section forever.

## 5. Earn READY — `tailrocks-finalize`

```text
Use tailrocks-finalize on offline-inspection.
```

Five screens have to be confirmed as schematic mockups, not adjectives: the
inspection runner with a per-item sync-state chip, the sync-center drawer with
its "safe to close" indicator, the live site board with a per-device staleness
badge, the conflict review with side-by-side verdicts and both timestamps, and
sign-off blocked on unresolved conflicts or unuploaded evidence.

Then the flow is walked end to end, including the ugly path: two inspectors,
one asset, contradictory verdicts, one device still in a basement when the
supervisor opens the board.

Anything still open at the end is resolved, deferred with a reason, or
reclassified as researchable. READY is granted only when the whole checklist
passes.

## 6. Package — `tailrocks-plan`

```text
Use tailrocks-plan on offline-inspection with --deep.
```

**This is a brownfield item, and the plan reflects it.** There is no
verification-baseline slice, because the gates already exist — the plan records
the proven build, test, and lint commands instead of standing up new ones. Where
the existing baseline is doubtful, the audit belongs to the setup skills before
planning, not inside it:

```text
Use tailrocks-tanstack-project-setup to audit this app and remediate every
baseline violation.
```

The spec carries the contract between the two languages as requirements rather
than folklore:

| Concern | Decided shape |
|---|---|
| Document sync | WebSocket, binary frames, an opaque CRDT update inside a thin envelope carrying inspection, device, operation id, device time, and schema version |
| Reconnect | Client sends a state vector and receives a delta, never a full document |
| Supervisor stream | Server-sent events with a monotonic id per event, heartbeats, and resume by last event id — plus an explicit "gap, resubscribe from snapshot" event, because the spec does not define what happens when that id is no longer available |
| Evidence | A separate resumable HTTP channel; the document holds a content hash and a blob handle, and the reference stays `pending` until bytes land |
| Types | One Rust source of truth, TypeScript generated from it |
| Errors | `application/problem+json` over HTTP and a typed error frame over the socket sharing one vocabulary, each error classed retryable, terminal, or needs-human |

That last row is not bureaucracy: the offline queue's entire retry behavior is a
function of the error class, so leaving it unspecified means the queue's most
important behavior gets invented by whoever writes it first.

The slices, in dependency order:

| Slice | Ships | Executed under |
|---|---|---|
| 001 | Document model, wire types, validation core in Rust with a conformance vector suite the TypeScript validator must also pass | `tailrocks-rust-best-practices`, `tailrocks-typescript-best-practices` |
| 002 | Offline runner, local only: local replica, persistence, storage telemetry, capture UI, photo staging | `tailrocks-typescript-best-practices` |
| 003 | Sync channel: WebSocket endpoint, server replica, state-vector backfill, dual-timestamp acks | `tailrocks-axum-best-practices`, `tailrocks-rust-best-practices` |
| 004 | Resumable evidence pipeline and reconciliation of pending references | `tailrocks-axum-best-practices` |
| 005 | Live site board: event fan-out, resume, gap event, staleness model | both stacks |
| 006 | Conflict detection, supervisor adjudication, sign-off gating, certificate | both stacks |

Two details in that ordering are deliberate.

**Slice 001 puts the validation rules in one place with a shared test suite.**
The rules run twice — in TypeScript for immediate offline feedback, in Rust
authoritatively at ingest — and two implementations of the same rules drift. The
drift surfaces as rejected inspections after the inspector has left the site, so
the conformance vectors are the fix, and they exist before either implementation
does.

**Slice 002 ships value alone**, with no server work at all, and proves the
durability findings on real devices before anything depends on them. If the
storage assumptions are wrong, that is the cheapest possible moment to learn it.

Each plan is written by its own subagent and read by a fresh-context reviewer
holding only that plan and the repository.

```text
+ plans/offline-inspection/coverage.md
+ plans/offline-inspection/spec/
+ plans/offline-inspection/README.md
+ plans/offline-inspection/001-*.md … 006-*.md
+ plans/offline-inspection/GOAL.md
~ roadmap/offline-inspection/README.md   Status: PLANNED
```

## 7. Execute and reconcile

Paste the goal condition and kickoff prompt from `GOAL.md`. One plan per
iteration, every verification run, status writes committed with the work.

Then the part this feature makes vivid. The research recorded two facts that are
true today and load-bearing tomorrow, and the ledger carries them as `A#`
assumptions rather than as settled truth:

- the upload client library the evidence pipeline leans on is maintained;
- no cross-browser API exists for prioritising which stored data survives
  eviction, so the storage-pressure behavior has to be a product decision.

```text
Use tailrocks-reconcile on offline-inspection.
```

Reconcile retests those assumptions along with everything else. If the upload
library went unmaintained between planning and slice 004, the row goes `STALE`
with a reason instead of an executor discovering it mid-implementation and
improvising a replacement. If a browser shipped the missing storage API, that is
a decision to revisit, not a silent improvement.

That is the whole argument for the pipeline in one paragraph: the facts that
justified a design are written down with their sources, so when the world moves,
you find out from a status row rather than from production.

## The contrast worth noticing

The [macOS example](/docs/delivery/macos-app) is greenfield, and its risk lives
in a boundary between two languages that has to be proven early — so the plan
opens with a verification baseline and a tracer bullet.

This one is brownfield, and its risk lives in the outside world: platform
storage rules, protocol maturity, library maintenance. So the weight moves to
`tailrocks-research`, and the plan's job is to keep those findings attached to
the work as assumptions that get retested rather than facts that quietly expire.

Same seven skills, same order, completely different centre of gravity. Choosing
where the effort goes is the judgement; the pipeline is what makes either choice
survive contact with an agent.
